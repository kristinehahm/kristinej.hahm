About

I am a Research Fellow at Georgetown University's Better Government Lab. I am also a Ph.D. candidate in Public Policy and Political Economy at the University of Texas at Dallas, where I also earned an M.S. in Geospatial Information Sciences. I hold a Master of Public Policy from Seoul National University. My research examines how administrative burden and geographic access jointly shape participation in safety-net programs and the receipt of health care among low-income populations.

My dissertation, The Pathways of Cancer Care: Knowledge, Administrative Burden, and Geography, links SEER cancer registry records with Medicaid administrative data to estimate how enrollment barriers and proximity to oncology care affect coverage, treatment, and survival, and how the Medicaid expansion under the ACA reshaped these relationships. I draw on causal inference, machine learning, and spatial data science.
