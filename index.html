About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Public Policy and Political Economy at the University of Texas at Dallas, where I also earned an M.S. in Geospatial Information Sciences. I hold a Master of Public Policy from Seoul National University. My research examines the structural and spatial determinants of health, with a focus on how administrative systems, geography, and policy shape access to cancer care among low-income and Medicaid-enrolled populations.

My dissertation, The Pathways of Cancer Care: Knowledge, Administrative Burden, and Geography, links SEER cancer registry records with Medicaid enrollment data to study how administrative burden and geographic access jointly shape cancer outcomes — and how Medicaid expansion under the ACA has reshaped that landscape. A second line of work uses geographically weighted models to examine antibiotic prescribing behavior in the United States.

I draw on causal inference, machine learning, and spatial data science, and I work primarily in R, with QGIS and ArcGIS for cartography.
